# Design: Making It Feel Like the Shop on Broadway

The goal: a storefront that feels like Campus Customs, a family-run Yale shop with a print shop next door, rather than a generic template. Every visual here is HTML/CSS/SVG or a real catalogue photo. Nothing is AI-generated.

## What changed and why it helps people stay and buy

| Area | What changed | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| **Type** | Headlines in **Libre Caslon** (a classic serif in the spirit of Yale's traditional look), **Graduate** varsity lettering used *only* for patches, labels, and prices, and **Inter** for body text. All self-hosted from npm, so there are no outside font requests. | Instantly reads as "Yale shop", not "online template". Varsity type is kept to small labels so body text stays easy to read. |
| **Color** | Yale blue, white, and grays only. Red is reserved for urgency: "Only 2 left", sold out. | The brand stays calm and familiar, so the one urgent color stands out when stock is low. |
| **Stitched details** | Dashed "stitching" along the nav and footer edges, felt-patch labels ("EST. 1975"), and a sewn-on felt **Y** patch. | Echoes the shop's own screen printing and embroidery, which is what makes Campus Customs different from a big-box store. |
| **Home hero** | A **folded stack of real products** that fans out on hover like a shop table, plus trust badges (Officially licensed · Printed on Broadway · XS–XXL). | Shows real merch in the first second instead of a wall of text. The badges answer "is this legit, and will it fit?" before anyone asks. |
| **Home sections** | A ticker ("Boola Boola · Bulldog Blue since 1975 · 57 Broadway…"), **category tiles** with live style counts, **residential-college pennants** (11 colleges that link straight to their gear), a **"The Game" band** styled like a football field with the Harvard–Yale tee, and a **striped shop awning** for the visit section. | Several ways in for different shoppers: by garment, by college pride (a big reason people buy here), or by the season's biggest event. The old home page had one grid. |
| **"Heavy" product cards** | On hover the card lifts slowly with a deep, layered shadow and a Yale-blue base. The photo leans in. The price hangs on a **clothing tag that swings**. A strip slides up showing **which sizes are in stock** (sold-out sizes crossed out). | Feels like picking up a real garment, which is the premium feel. The size strip answers the #1 question ("do they have my size?") without a click, so fewer people open a product only to be disappointed. |
| **Handsome Dan (2 places only)** | 1) **The chat assistant**: Dan is the avatar, pops his head up when you hover "Ask Dan", and shows as "Dan is checking the stockroom…" with bouncing paw prints while he looks things up. 2) **Create Account**: Dan slides out from under the nav bar, paws on the edge, when you hover the button. Drawn in SVG. | A mascot face makes the assistant feel friendly enough to try. People engage more with a character than with a generic "chat" box, and the chat is where alternatives and saved sales happen. Dan on Create Account is a small reward on the button the business most wants clicked. We kept him to two places so he stays charming, not gimmicky. |
| **Tuck-away launcher** | "Ask Dan" shows in full for 4 seconds on a shopper's first page of the visit, then **tucks into just Dan's face, partly off the right edge**. It slides back out to the full button on hover or keyboard focus, and one click opens the chat. | The full button was covering product cards and links. Tucked, it stays out of the way while people browse, but it's always one move away, and the first-visit intro makes sure nobody misses it. |
| **Chat feel** | Bubbles with a flat "tail" corner, Dan's avatar on his replies, timestamps, a live green "online" dot, a panel that springs open from the corner, and rounded input. | It looks like the messaging apps people already use, so it's obvious how it works. |
| **Search results fly in from the chat** | When Dan finds products, the cards **fly from the chat corner onto the page** and land in place. Dan's reply says "↖ 3 shown on the page". | Makes the connection visible: you see the assistant put them there. That builds trust that the results came from the shop's stock. |
| **Motion system** | One "heavy" easing curve for lifts and a spring curve for Dan and the chat. Grids fade up in a quick stagger. Buttons lift slightly on hover. | Consistent, physical-feeling motion makes the site feel polished and responsive, so it reads as a store you can trust. |
| **Product & account pages** | Varsity price and size labels, sold-out sizes crossed out, pages that rise in, and a Create Account card with a Yale-blue top edge and soft shadow. | The same look carries through checkout-adjacent pages, so nothing feels unfinished. |

## Guardrails

- **Accessible motion:** with the OS "reduce motion" setting on, all animation is switched off (the ticker stops). Every feature still works.
- **Honest content:** the ticker, badges, and copy only state facts from the store research and the database (1975, 57 Broadway, in-house printing, XS–XXL). There are no fake sales or reviews.
- **The prompt knows about Dan:** the agent may call itself Dan but must never claim to be a person or an official Yale account (`prompts/prompt.md` → Voice).
